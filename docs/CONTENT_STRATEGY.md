## Content Pillars
1. **Recognition** — name the reader's exact internal pattern with specific, oddly-precise detail (not generic "overthinking is hard") so they feel *seen*, not sold to. This is the highest-performing hook style right now per research — lead with it.
2. **Emilie's story** — anxiety/depression/panic → trying everything without lasting change → the subconscious/nervous-system shift. Her lived experience is a core trust asset; use it often, in pieces, not just once.
3. **Education** — nervous-system / subconscious-pattern explanations of why overthinking, people-pleasing, and self-doubt happen (not just how to "fix" them) — ties to her method without naming the certification lineage (see `docs/BRAND.md` positioning decision).
4. **Client transformation** — testimonials and before/after language (self-doubt → self-trust), see `My profile/testimonials.md`.
5. **Offer/CTA** — "Come Home to Yourself" program and the free Clarity Call, used sparingly relative to the above four.

## Content Types
- Raw, minimal-edit talking-head Reels with on-screen text/subtitles — primary format (outperforming polished content per research)
- Carousels for education pillar
- Occasional demonstrative clips (a somatic/regulation technique shown on camera) — borrowed from what's working on TikTok
- In-person event recap content, once in-person offer exists

## Hook Guidance (from research)
- Lead with specific, identifiable scenarios: "if a delayed text reorganizes your entire afternoon, that's not overreacting — that's a nervous system doing its job badly" — style hooks, not generic promises ("heal your nervous system").
- Avoid abstract mindset lines ("your mind is your greatest asset") — these no longer perform.
- Hook must land in the first 1.5 seconds — don't build up to the point.

## Repurposing Workflow
- One raw Reel → reused as-is on TikTok (same raw style works on both) → key line pulled into a carousel or caption for Instagram.
