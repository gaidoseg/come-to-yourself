## 1. RECOGNITION
**Pillar:** Recognition | **Platform:** Instagram (reusable as-is on TikTok) | **Format:** Raw talking-head, on-screen text | **Hook type:** Recognition hook (specific/oddly-precise scenario)

**Hook (0–1.5s, also on-screen as bold text overlay):**
> "If you've re-read your own text three times before hitting send — and you still wonder if it sounded 'too much' — that's not overthinking."

**Body (spoken, ~40s):**
That's a nervous system checking if it's safe for you to just... exist out loud.

Because somewhere along the way, you learned that being "too much" or "too little" had consequences. So your brain started pre-editing you. Before anyone else even got the chance to judge you — you already did it for them.

[On-screen text: "overthinking isn't a personality trait"]

And that's exhausting, right? You're not lazy, you're not "bad at relaxing." You are running a 24/7 safety scan on every word, every message, every silence after you hit send.

[On-screen text: "it's not your mind being dramatic — it's your body trying to protect you"]

Here's the part nobody tells you: you can't think your way out of a pattern your body built to keep you safe. You have to teach your nervous system — not just your mind — that it's actually safe to be seen.

**On-screen text cues:**
- 0:00 — "If you re-read your own texts 3x before sending..."
- mid — "overthinking isn't a personality trait"
- mid — "it's not your mind being dramatic — it's your body trying to protect you"
- end — "you can't think your way out of a body pattern"

**CTA (spoken + on-screen):**
"If this is you — drop a 🫠 below. I talk about this stuff here often."

## 3. EDUCATION
**Pillar:** Education | **Platform:** Instagram (reusable as-is on TikTok) | **Format:** Raw talking-head, on-screen text | **Hook type:** Recognition hook leading into explanation

**Hook (0–1.5s, on-screen text):**
> "If you say yes when your whole body is screaming no — that's not people-pleasing. That's a learned survival response."

**Body (spoken, ~45s):**
People-pleasing gets talked about like it's a character flaw. Like you're just "too nice" or "need to set better boundaries." But that's not what's actually happening.

[On-screen text: "it's called a fawn response"]

Somewhere, your nervous system learned that keeping other people happy was the safest move — that if you disappointed someone, something bad might happen. Not logically. Physically. In your body.

So now, even when you consciously want to say no, your body still floods with the same alarm it used as a kid: *don't upset them, stay small, stay safe.* That's not weakness. That's a nervous system doing exactly what it was trained to do.

[On-screen text: "you can't boundary your way out of a nervous system pattern"]

This is why reading another book about boundaries doesn't fix it — because the pattern isn't living in your logic, it's living in your body. You have to actually go back and show your nervous system, on a felt level, that it's safe to disappoint someone and still be okay.

**On-screen text cues:**
- 0:00 — "Saying yes when your body screams no..."
- early — "it's called a fawn response"
- mid — "your body floods with: don't upset them, stay small, stay safe"
- late — "you can't boundary your way out of a nervous system pattern"

**CTA (spoken + on-screen):**
"Save this one for the next time you're about to say yes and mean no."
